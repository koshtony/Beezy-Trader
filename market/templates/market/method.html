Methodology

1 · Data

  • Daily prices: the NSE end-of-day price list (one request to afx.kwayisi.org), stored each trading day with volume.
  • History: about ten years of daily closes per counter, loaded once. The site's 60-second crawl delay is respected.
  • Fundamentals: trailing-twelve-month revenue, gross profit, net income, EPS (and its multi-year history), equity, debt, operating cash flow, capex, dividends and analyst targets from stockanalysis.com. Refreshed weekly.
  • News: Google News (Kenya edition), searched for each company's name over the last 60 days.

2 · Metrics

P/E, PEG, P/B, P/S, dividend yield and payout are recalculated with today's price. ROE, gross margin, debt-to-equity and free cash flow come from the latest reported figures. Each metric gets a verdict (good fair poor) using common rules of thumb and the median for its sector. Banks and insurers are treated differently: free cash flow and gross margin don't apply to them, and their debt-to-equity counts only borrowings, not customer deposits.

3 · Projections

Short horizons lean on recent behaviour, and recent headline sentiment nudges the daily and weekly projections. Longer horizons lean on long-run history and on fundamentals. The fundamental anchor is expected EPS growth plus a partial re-rating toward the sector's median P/E. Where analysts publish a price target, it is blended in too. The ranges are 90% log-normal bands built from a mix of recent (EWMA) and longer-run volatility. Every projection is backtested walk-forward: we report how often it called the direction correctly and how often the actual price landed inside the band. Day-to-day price moves are close to random, so expect daily direction calls of only about 50%.

Risk (0–100) combines volatility, maximum drawdown, beta, thin trading, leverage and losses. The risk-adjusted score is the composite score minus 0.2 points for every risk point above 50. The Top 5 are the highest risk-adjusted scores among liquid, profitable ordinary shares with a positive 12-month projection, with at most two per sector. High risk · high gain takes the counters with above-median risk and ranks them by projected return. Low risk · steady ranks counters scoring at least 45 by lowest risk.

5 · Portfolio calculator

Choosing the stocks. Only counters that qualify for the Top 5 are considered: liquid, profitable ordinary shares with a positive 12-month projection.

  • Low risk takes the lowest-risk counters scoring at least 45 (and at or below the median risk), weighted toward the least volatile.
  • Steady takes the best risk-adjusted scores, weighted by score.
  • High risk takes above-median-risk counters with the largest projected gains, weighted by projected gain.

Every style holds at most two counters per sector, and each weight stays between 8% and 35%.

Buying. The calculator buys whole shares and charges about 2.1% in trading costs (brokerage plus NSE, CMA and CDSC levies). It charges the same again when you sell.

Simulating. It runs 4,000 simulations in which the counters move together the way they have over the last year.

  • Each counter's typical path follows its daily, weekly, monthly and yearly projections.
  • Because the calculator deliberately picks the counters with the best projections, and those estimates tend to be too high, each pick's path is pulled halfway toward a 7% a year market price return first.
  • Every simulation also draws its own error in the projected return (±15% a year), because the projections themselves are uncertain.
  • Beyond one year, growth fades toward each company's long-run price trend and the 7% anchor, and is capped at 10% a year.

Dividends. Today's dividend per share is added pro rata, less 5% withholding tax.

The calculator reports the median outcome (half the simulations did better, half worse), the 5th and 95th percentiles, and the chance of ending above the amount you put in.

This is not investment advice. Models are simplifications, NSE data can be stale or thinly traded, and past returns don't predict future ones.
